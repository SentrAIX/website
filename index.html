A testing buddy built for teams that ship every week

Four things TestAIx does on every release, so your people don't have to.

Walks real journeys

Signup to paid, invite to permissions. TestAIx moves through your app like a customer, in a real browser.

Keeps tests current

When a button moves or a label changes, affected tests are revisited instead of breaking. You approve the change.

Brings evidence

Every run returns screenshots, steps and network clues. Nobody has to trust a summary.

Leaves the call to you

TestAIx never ships anything. A named person on your team holds or releases.

Every check was green. The upgrade still broke.

Unit tests look at each screen on its own. Customers move between them, and that's where the painful bugs hide: payment to access, invite to permissions, signup to a usable workspace.

TestAIx tests the whole path in a real browser with your test accounts. It's the check that sits between your unit tests and your customers.

We build tools for QA teams. Your people keep the final call.

Testing needs people who ask awkward questions and are willing to say "not yet". TestAIx takes the reruns, the broken selectors and the screenshot collecting, so your people have time for that.

TestAIx handles

  1. Clicking through the same forty screens before each release

  2. Fixing tests when a button moves or a label changes

  3. Overnight and scheduled reruns

  4. Collecting screenshots and step logs

Your team keeps

  1. Deciding which journeys matter this sprint

  2. Spotting the edge case nobody wrote down

  3. Working out if a failure is a real bug

  4. The release call. Always.

That's why there's no per-seat plan. Adding a tester to your team should never add a line to your bill.

Nobody outside the team ever knew.

    A billing change gets merged

    Engineering
  2. 10:20

    The scheduled staging run signs up a new member, picks Pro and pays with a test card.

    TestAIx
  3. 10:31

    It catches the gap

    Payment succeeds, but the account still says Free after 30 seconds. Screenshots and network clues go to the release review.

    TestAIx
  4. 10:40

    The QA lead holds the release

    One look at the evidence is enough. No meeting, no argument about whether it is real.

    QA lead
  5. 11:15

    The fix is a one-line change

    The engineer sees the webhook never updated the plan. Fixed, reviewed, merged.

    Engineering
  6. 14:05

    The rerun passes. It ships.

    All four journeys pass. The release goes out the same afternoon, and support never hears about it.
